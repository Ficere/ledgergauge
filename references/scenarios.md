# Scenario Design: One Platform, Two Recognition Calibers

The single most important framing rule: build the report as **one platform under two
recognition calibers**, NOT as two competing plans or "versions." This keeps the document
internally self-consistent and avoids version-comparison clutter.

## What is shared (identical across scenarios)

- The financing & repayment schedule (principal, rate, term, equal-installment plan)
- Layer A (recurring) revenue, year by year
- Layer B (co-development) revenue, year by year
- The gross-margin structure, cash opex, depreciation, working-capital ratio
- The sensitivity framework

## What differs (the only divergence)

Only **Layer C (IP / licensing) recognition**:

| Scenario | Layer-C recognition | Reading |
|----------|---------------------|---------|
| **Conservative** | Probability-weighted (≈30–40% of nominal) | Cash-flow **floor** — what survives if the lumpy deals slip |
| **Optimistic** | Full (100%) | Upside **elasticity** — what happens if deals land on time and in full |

State explicitly: *"the entire gap between the two ends comes from whether the lumpy
licensing deals land on schedule; the real outcome most likely sits between them."*

## Presentation pattern

1. One full dual-caliber table per scenario (Section 4.x each).
2. A compact side-by-side **key-metrics summary** (breakeven, payback, cumulative net income,
   cash trough) so the reader sees the spread at a glance.
3. A one-line interpretation tying the spread back to Layer-C execution risk.

## Naming hygiene

- Pick neutral scenario names (e.g. "conservative" / "optimistic", or "downside" / "base" / "upside").
- Do not embed any sensitive label, funding-programme name, counterparty, or internal codename
  in the scenario names or anywhere in the report.
- Avoid cross-referencing other documents by a title that itself leaks sensitive information.
